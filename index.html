        Why competitors can't easily copy this

        The moat comes from the care network and the data, not the band.

        1. 1
          Licensed Kenyan care networkNurses and partner-hospital doctors take years to build. Apple and Whoop don't run clinics in Nairobi.
        2. 2
          Data rights we ownOur own band means no Apple HealthKit limits on sharing data with insurers.
        3. 3
          Insurer switching costsInsurers' group-risk models are trained on Mlinzi data, so leaving us is costly.
        4. 4
          Path down-marketLower claims fund a cheaper tier for the middle class, then expansion across East Africa.

        Biggest threat

        An insurer like Jubilee or Britam builds its own nurse line and hands out off-the-shelf bands. Our answer: we're cheaper than building it themselves, and we bring the data history.

        Hardest question

        "I already wear an Apple Watch." The Mlinzi band is the entry ticket to the care team, and the insurer discount roughly offsets the Guardian subscription.

        What we don't claim

        The band doesn't diagnose. Clinical decisions use cuff readings and doctors. That keeps the device out of medical-device registration with Kenya's Pharmacy and Poisons Board in phase one.
