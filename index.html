    Conditions caught early

    The rule in every insurer contract

    Member data is never used to raise one person's premium or deny cover. Pricing uses group numbers only. Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019 treats health data as sensitive.

    Members can opt in to share their record for one purpose: faster care approval, which raises their discount from 8% to 15%.

    Why it's hard to copy

    • A licensed Kenyan care networkNurses and partner-hospital doctors take years to build. Apple and Whoop don't run clinics in Nairobi.
    • Data rights we ownOur own band means no Apple HealthKit limits on sharing data with insurers.
    • Insurer switching costsInsurers' group-risk models are trained on Mlinzi data, so leaving us is costly.
    • A path down-marketLower claims fund a cheaper tier for the middle class, then expansion across East Africa.

    Questions we expect

    • "I already wear an Apple Watch."The Mlinzi band is the entry ticket to the care team, and the insurer discount roughly offsets the Guardian subscription.
    • "Can't an insurer build this itself?"Jubilee or Britam could run a nurse line with off-the-shelf bands. We're cheaper than building it, and we bring the data history.
    • "Is the band a medical device?"It doesn't diagnose. Clinical decisions use cuff readings and doctors, which keeps it out of Pharmacy and Poisons Board registration in phase one.
